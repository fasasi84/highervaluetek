The operating problem

When the warning is buried, downtime gets expensive.

Maintenance teams often have the clues, but they are spread across logs, inspections, spreadsheets and people’s memory.

01

Unplanned interruptions

Equipment failures can stop production, disrupt service commitments and force urgent parts or labor decisions.

02

Fragmented maintenance records

Work orders and inspection notes are difficult to compare when they live in disconnected formats and systems.

03

Reactive prioritization

Teams need a practical way to decide which asset deserves attention first, without replacing the judgment of experienced technicians.

AI technology

Useful signals, not a black box.

AI is intended to help maintenance teams find patterns across records and telemetry. Model choices and performance targets will be validated with pilot data before any operational claims are made.

Data inputs

Planned inputs include work orders, inspection notes, asset metadata and condition or operating time-series where customers can provide them.

What it may detect

Repeated fault descriptions, unusual changes in sensor patterns and assets whose maintenance history warrants review.

Methods under evaluation

Natural-language processing for maintenance text, anomaly detection and time-series analysis. Specific model architectures remain subject to data quality and pilot validation.

Human oversight

Recommendations are intended as decision support. Technicians and reliability leads remain responsible for inspections, work authorization and safety decisions.

How it is intended to work

Start with the records teams already have.

Connect a pilot dataset

Agree on a narrow asset group and review available records, access permissions and data quality.

Build a usable asset history

Normalize equipment identifiers, service events and inspection findings into a common timeline.

Surface patterns for review

Apply validated analysis to highlight repeat issues and unusual signals with supporting evidence.

Measure and refine

Review outputs with maintenance staff and track agreed pilot measures before considering expansion.

Technology & infrastructure

Cloud and accelerated computing, applied where they help.

Infrastructure choices are planned, not yet deployed. The pilot architecture will be sized around data sensitivity, latency, workload and customer requirements.

Planned AWS foundation

AWS services are being evaluated for encrypted object storage, managed relational data, API hosting, identity controls, monitoring and model integration. Amazon S3, RDS, ECS or Lambda, CloudFront, Bedrock and CloudWatch are candidate services, subject to architecture and security review.

S3 / secure filesRDS / asset recordsECS or Lambda / APIsCloudWatch / monitoring

Planned NVIDIA evaluation

GPU acceleration may support experimentation on larger maintenance datasets and faster model inference if pilot workloads justify it. CUDA, TensorRT and Triton Inference Server are candidates for optimizing validated models; smaller workloads may run efficiently without dedicated GPUs.

Why now

Industrial teams need to make more of the data they already collect.

Digital maintenance records and connected equipment are producing more operational information, but useful decisions still depend on bringing the pieces together. A carefully validated AI workflow can help teams focus attention without removing people from the loop.
